## Overview

Teotihuacan is located in the Valley of Mexico, approximately 40 kilometers northeast of modern Mexico City, in the State of Mexico (coordinates: 19°41'33"N, 98°50'38"W). At its height between 400 and 500 CE, it was among the largest cities in the ancient world, with an estimated population of 100,000 to 200,000 people — comprising 80–90% of the Valley of Mexico's population — making it the sixth-largest city in the world at that time. The city covers approximately 8 to 21 square kilometers and features a central 4-kilometer boulevard (the Avenue of the Dead, 40 meters wide), the massive Pyramid of the Sun (third-largest pyramid in the world by base area: 219.4 × 231.6 meters, 65 meters tall), the Pyramid of the Moon, and the Temple of the Feathered Serpent (Quetzalcoatl) with its associated underground tunnel system. The city's builders and original ethnic identity remain subjects of scholarly debate; candidates include Nahuas, Otomi, Totonac, or a multi-ethnic founding population. Uniquely among ancient civilizations, there is no evidence of a centralized monarchy at Teotihuacan: no royal palaces, no ceremonial ball courts, and no depictions of conquest or individual rulers in the artistic record, suggesting some form of collective governance. The city declined around 550–650 CE, with evidence of burning concentrated on elite structures (suggesting internal uprising or civil strife rather than external conquest), correlated with climate changes of 535–536 CE and possible drought-induced famine. The city was rediscovered and named by the Aztecs (Mexica) centuries after its abandonment; the Nahuatl name Teōtīhuacān means 'birthplace of the gods.' It was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987.

## Etymology

- **Origin**: Nahuatl language (Aztec), applied to the already-abandoned city when the Aztecs encountered it.
- **Meaning**: 'Birthplace of the gods' (Nahuatl: teōtl = god + ihuan = among + can = place). A 2018 scholarly proposal suggests the original name may have been 'Teohuacan' meaning 'City of the Sun.' The Maya texts reference the city as 'Puh' (Place of Reeds).
- **First use**: The Nahuatl name was applied by the Aztecs after approximately 1300 CE. The original name used by the city's inhabitants is unknown.

## Physical Description

- **Morphology**: The city covered 8–21 square kilometers and contained approximately 2,000 residential apartment compounds housing its population of up to 200,000. Its layout reflects precise astronomical and calendrical planning. Multi-ethnic residential neighborhoods have been documented, including a Oaxacan (Zapotec) district called Tlailotlacan and a Gulf Coast traders' barrio.

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Divine or extraterrestrial construction** _(Ancient astronaut theorists (Erich von Däniken tradition))_

Popular but unsupported fringe hypothesis: the pyramids' scale and astronomical precision suggest construction by beings beyond ordinary human capability — either divine beings (Aztec interpretation) or extraterrestrial visitors.

### Scientific

**Multi-ethnic collective governance city-state** _(Mainstream Mesoamerican archaeologists, Sergio Gómez Chávez, Saburo Sugiyama)_

Mainstream archaeological interpretation: Teotihuacan was built and governed by a multi-ethnic population using some form of collective or distributed governance rather than centralized monarchy. The absence of royal palaces, royal tombs, and conquest imagery supports this interpretation.

**Climate-driven collapse** _(Archaeological climate researchers)_

The city's decline around 550–650 CE corresponds to documented climate disruptions of 535–536 CE (possibly caused by volcanic eruptions), which may have caused droughts, crop failures, and famine leading to social unrest and eventual abandonment.

**Internal uprising** _(Linda Manzanilla, other Mesoamerican archaeologists)_

The selective burning of elite compounds and temples — but not the residential compounds of ordinary residents — suggests the city's decline was triggered by internal revolt against an elite class, rather than foreign conquest.
